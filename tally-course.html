Big 4 & CA Audit Firm Technical Interview Bank 4 Core Questions
Q1 What are the Golden Rules of Accounting, and what is the dual aspect concept?
▾

The Golden Rules are: (1) Personal Account: Debit the Receiver, Credit the Giver. (2) Real Account: Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out. (3) Nominal Account: Debit all expenses & losses, Credit all incomes & gains. The Dual Aspect Concept states that every business transaction affects at least two accounts in opposite directions such that Assets = Liabilities + Capital at all times.

Q2 What is the key difference between an Error of Principle and an Error of Commission?
▾

An Error of Principle occurs when fundamental accounting principles are violated—such as treating a capital expenditure as revenue expenditure (e.g., debiting Repairs Account instead of Machinery Account for installation). It does not affect the Trial Balance totals. An Error of Commission is a clerical mistake, such as incorrect totaling (casting error), posting to the wrong side of an account, or writing the wrong amount, which often creates a Trial Balance mismatch.

Q3 Why is Straight Line Method (SLM) different from Written Down Value (WDV) under Income Tax Act?
▾

Under SLM, depreciation is charged at a fixed percentage on the original historical cost every year, reducing the book value to zero or scrap value. Under WDV, depreciation is calculated on the declining opening written down balance each year, so depreciation amount decreases progressively. Under Section 32 of the Indian Income Tax Act 1961, depreciation on asset blocks is mandatory on WDV basis (except for power generating units).

Q4 How do you treat Trade Discount versus Cash Discount in accounting entries?
▾

Trade Discount is a price concession granted at the time of purchase based on catalog or volume. It is deducted directly on the face of the invoice and is NEVER recorded separately in accounting ledger books. Cash Discount is an incentive granted to encourage prompt settlement within a stipulated credit period; it is recorded as a separate Nominal Account ('Discount Allowed' as expense or 'Discount Received' as income).

Q1 What is the difference between Primary Groups and Secondary Groups in TallyPrime?
▾

In TallyPrime, out of 28 predefined groups, 15 are Primary Groups that form the root structure of financial statements (9 appear on the Balance Sheet like Capital, Fixed Assets, Current Assets; 6 appear on the Profit & Loss statement like Sales, Purchase, Direct/Indirect Expenses). The remaining 13 are Secondary (Sub-groups) that are pre-linked to Primary groups (e.g., Sundry Debtors and Bank Accounts belong to Current Assets, Reserves & Surplus belongs to Capital Account).

Q2 What happens if 'Financial Year From' and 'Books Beginning From' dates are different in Tally?
▾

Financial Year From represents the statutory financial year (e.g., 01-04-2026 to 31-03-2027). Books Beginning From is the actual date on which the business commenced operations or switched to Tally (e.g., 01-10-2026). Tally allows recording transactions only on or after the 'Books Beginning From' date, while closing statements and depreciation calculations align with the statutory Financial Year cycle.

Q3 How does TallyPrime's 'Go To' (Alt+G) feature transform daily accounting productivity?
▾

The Go To feature provides omnipresent global search across the entire software without closing current vouchers. An accountant typing a complex 15-item sales invoice can press Alt+G, check customer outstanding ledgers, view stock availability in multiple godowns, print a pending balance sheet, and press Esc to return seamlessly to the exact same cursor position in the voucher without losing data.

Q4 Can you delete a ledger account in TallyPrime that has existing transactions?
▾

No. TallyPrime strictly prohibits deleting any ledger (or stock item) that has voucher transactions posted to it, preserving financial audit integrity. To delete a ledger (Alt+D in Alter mode), all vouchers associated with that ledger must first be deleted or reassigned to another ledger account.

Big 4 & CA Audit Firm Technical Interview Bank 4 Core Questions
Q1 What is the difference between Stock Groups and Stock Categories in TallyPrime?
▾

Stock Group classifies items based on their core nature or department (e.g., 'Television', 'Air Conditioner', 'Raw Materials'). Stock Category provides a cross-cutting parallel classification based on common attributes across different groups (e.g., 'Sony', 'Samsung', '55-inch Screen', or 'Imported vs Domestic'). This allows generating reports filtered by brand regardless of whether the item is a TV, soundbar, or monitor.

Q2 How does Bill of Materials (BOM) in TallyPrime calculate the final unit cost of a manufactured item?
▾

In TallyPrime's Manufacturing Journal, BOM calculates the unit cost by summing: (1) Total cost of raw materials consumed from source godowns based on standard valuation, minus the value of any by-products or scrap generated, plus (2) Additional operational expenses (direct wages, machinery power, factory rent) added as percentage or flat amount. The total cost is divided by total finished units produced to determine the exact finished inventory cost.

Q3 What is the function of the Physical Stock Voucher (Ctrl+F7)?
▾

The Physical Stock Voucher is used to record the actual physical stock counted during warehouse audits. When physical quantities differ from book balances (due to shrinkage, pilferage, evaporation, or unrecorded damage), entering the actual count into this voucher forces TallyPrime to adjust stock balances automatically, posting the inventory discrepancy to the Profit & Loss statement.

Q4 Explain how compound units of measurement work with an example.
▾

A compound unit represents a relationship between two simple units. For example, a wholesale distributor selling chocolate bars creates simple units 'Box' and 'Pcs'. The compound unit is created as 'Box of 24 Pcs'. When a customer buys 38 Pcs, Tally records it as 1 Box and 14 Pcs, automatically calculating the invoice amount accurately based on per-box or per-piece pricing.

An Intrastate supply occurs when the supplier's location and the Place of Supply are within the same State or Union Territory; it attracts CGST (paid to the Central Government) and SGST/UTGST (paid to the State Government) in equal 50:50 proportions. An Interstate supply occurs when the supplier's location and the Place of Supply are in different States or Union Territories (or imports/exports); it attracts IGST (Integrated GST) collected by the Centre and apportioned to the consuming State under destination-based taxation.

Q2 Explain the mandatory E-Invoicing rule in India and how TallyPrime implements it.
▾

Under GST regulations, businesses with an aggregate annual turnover exceeding ₹5 Crore in any preceding financial year must generate E-Invoices for all B2B transactions, Credit Notes, Debit Notes, and exports. TallyPrime integrates directly with the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) via GSP/API. Upon saving a sales voucher, Tally uploads invoice JSON, receives a 64-character Invoice Reference Number (IRN), acknowledgement number, and digitally signed QR code, and prints them directly on the tax invoice.

Q3 What is Section 17(5) of the CGST Act regarding Blocked Input Tax Credit (Ineligible ITC)?
▾

Section 17(5) lists specific goods and services on which Input Tax Credit cannot be claimed even if used for business purposes. Common blocked credits include: (1) Motor vehicles for passenger transport (capacity ≤ 13 persons), (2) Food and beverages, catering, outdoor dining, and beauty treatment, (3) Membership of a club, health, and fitness center, (4) Works contract services for construction of an immovable property (except plant & machinery), and (5) Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or disposed of as gifts.

Q4 How does the GST Input Tax Credit (ITC) utilization hierarchy work under Rule 88A?
▾

Under Rule 88A: (1) IGST credit must be utilized first completely towards IGST liability, and any remaining IGST credit can be utilized towards CGST and SGST in any order and proportion chosen by the taxpayer. (2) Only after the entire IGST credit pool is exhausted can CGST credit be utilized against CGST liability (and remaining towards IGST). (3) SGST credit is utilized against SGST liability (and remaining towards IGST). Note that CGST credit can NEVER be set off against SGST liability, and vice versa.

Q1 Why does the balance in the Cash Book (Bank column) differ from the Bank Pass Book?
▾

Discrepancies arise primarily due to two factors: (1) Timing differences: Cheques issued by the firm but not yet presented for payment by creditors (Pass Book shows higher balance); Cheques deposited into bank but not yet cleared/collected (Cash Book shows higher balance). Q3 What is the difference between Cash Flow and Funds Flow Statements?
▾

A Cash Flow Statement (prepared under AS-3 / Ind AS 7) tracks the inflows and outflows of cash and cash equivalents categorized into Operating, Investing, and Financing activities, reflecting short-term liquidity. A Funds Flow Statement tracks the change in overall working capital (Current Assets - Current Liabilities) between two balance sheet dates, highlighting long-term sources (e.g., share issuance, debentures) and applications (e.g., machinery purchase, loan repayment).

Q4 What does the Current Ratio indicate, and what is considered an ideal ratio for an Indian commercial firm?
▾

The Current Ratio (Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities) measures a company's ability to cover its short-term obligations due within one year using its short-term assets. A ratio of 2:1 (or 1.33:1 under bank Tandon Committee norms for working capital CC limits) is generally considered a healthy standard, indicating that the business possesses twice as many current assets as current liabilities.

▾

Division II applies to companies required to comply with the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules 2015 (Ind AS companies, such as listed companies and those with net worth ≥ ₹250 Crore), which follows IFRS convergence and fair value accounting.

Q2 What is Section 43B of the Income Tax Act and why is it critical during year-end final accounts?
▾

Section 43B specifies certain statutory deductions that are allowed ONLY on an actual payment basis, regardless of the method of accounting followed. Key payments covered include: any tax, duty, cess, or fee (such as GST); employer contribution to PF/ESI; bonus/commission to employees; interest on loans from banks/financial institutions; leave encashment; and sums payable to Micro and Small Enterprises under the MSMED Act within 45 days. If not paid before the ITR due date, they are added back to taxable income.

Q3 Why and when do you split company data in TallyPrime, and what precautions are needed?
▾

Splitting company data divides a multi-year company into separate financial year datasets (e.g., closing FY 2025-26 and opening FY 2026-27 as a fresh file). It reduces data file size, accelerates report loading, and prevents accidental alteration of audited past vouchers. Before splitting, an accountant must: (1) Take a verified backup, (2) Ensure unadjusted bills and negative stock balances are cleared, and (3) Verify data integrity using Tally's 'Verify Company Data' tool.

Q4 How does TallyPrime treat Foreign Exchange Gain or Loss under AS-11?
▾

When transactions involve foreign currencies (e.g., export sales booked at USD $1 = ₹83, but settlement received at USD $1 = ₹84), TallyPrime identifies the forex difference. The accountant records an adjustment voucher using the 'Forex Gain/Loss' voucher class in the Journal. Tally automatically calculates the unadjusted foreign exchange variance and posts it to 'Foreign Exchange Fluctuation Account' (recognized in P&L under AS-11).
